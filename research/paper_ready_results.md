# Paper-ready experimental findings

## Reproducible dataset

This report is generated by `research/scripts/analyze_results.py` from the committed raw archives in `research/raw/`. It reads the 110 sweep `.sca` files and 25 fixed-60-node `.sca` files, for **135 selected simulation repetitions** and **405 broadcast observations**. Each repetition is expected to contain three broadcasts. The fixed-size comparison uses 60 nodes and 10% malicious peers for attack configurations; baseline runs use 0%. The malicious-fraction sweep tests 5%, 20%, and 30% malicious participation at 60 nodes.

Five repetitions (seed sets 0–4) are present per condition. All tested simulations use complete-graph topologies. The main protocol parameters are fanout 4, 10 ms forwarding delay, 1 ms gossip delay, three broadcasts per repetition at 250 ms intervals, and a 20 s simulated-time limit.

**Metric definitions.** Mean coverage is the average percentage of nodes reached for individual broadcasts. Full-delivery rate is the percentage of broadcasts whose recorded `completionTime` is non-negative. Transmissions, drops, duplicates, and malicious actions are average counts per broadcast. Conditional T90/T95 means are computed only over broadcasts with a recorded non-negative threshold time. Blank T95 entries indicate older result files did not record that scalar; they are not imputed.

## Main observations supported by the recorded data

1. **Drop-All is the most damaging tested attack in the malicious-fraction sweep.** At 5% malicious participation, its mean coverage is 97.89% and full delivery is 13.33%. At 20% and 30%, mean coverage is 94.89% and 95.00%, and no broadcast in either condition delivers to every node. Mean recorded drops increase from 14.33 at 5% to 57.67 at 20% and 85.33 at 30%.
2. **Selective forwarding degrades as malicious participation increases.** Mean coverage falls from 97.89% at 5% to 96.22% at 30%, while full delivery falls from 33.33% to 13.33%.
3. **Delay/Jitter affects timeliness more than final coverage in this particular configuration.** Mean coverage changes from 98.22% at 5% to 98.44% at 30%; conditional mean T95 changes from 50.31 ms to 67.43 ms. The result should not be generalized beyond the implemented policy and complete-graph topology.
4. **Perfect delivery becomes less frequent at larger network sizes even when average coverage remains high.** Baseline mean coverage is 99.11%, 97.78%, and 97.87% at 30, 60, and 100 nodes, while full-delivery rates are 80.00%, 20.00%, and 0.00%, respectively. This shows why reporting coverage and complete delivery separately is important.
5. **Communication volume scales upward with network size in the complete-graph experiments.** Baseline mean transmissions rise from 118.93 at 30 nodes to 234.67 at 60 and 391.47 at 100. In the Drop-All fraction sweep, mean transmissions decrease as dropped forwards rise, which reflects suppressed dissemination rather than improved efficiency by itself.
6. **All recorded tree-validity scalars equal 1.** Structural validation passing is not equivalent to every node receiving the broadcast; delivery metrics show missed nodes in many observations.

## Limitations

- The experiments use complete-graph topologies; results should not be treated as evidence for sparse overlays or real production networks.
- Five repetitions per condition make this an exploratory simulation study. Avoid statistical-significance claims without additional repetitions and a pre-specified test.
- The fixed 60-node result files predate the `timeTo95pctReachable` scalar. These missing values remain blank instead of being estimated.
- Isolation results depend on the configured explicit peer set, and all conclusions are tied to the implemented attack policies and protocol parameters.
